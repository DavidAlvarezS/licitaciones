Scope National 
 
The Foundation for the Internationalisation of Public Administrations (FIAP, FSP) is a 
Spanish public sector foundation and an actor in Spanish and European cooperation 
whose work is based on knowledge exchange and peer learning between counterpart 
public administrations in different countries. Within the framework of Spanish foreign 
policy, FIAP supports the international activity of the Public Administration by promoting 
institutional capacity development in a range of sectors.   
FIAP has signed a delegation agreement to implement, as leader of a consortium with 
Italy and Slovakia, the project “EU Support for Internal Market” (EU4IM), which aims to 
promote Serbia’s alignment with the EU’s regulatory, institutional and operational 
frameworks in order to facilitate the integration of the Serbian market into the European 
single market.   
The project works to harmonise the country’s regulatory and operational framework with 
that of the EU in order to improve the capacity and efficiency of Serbian institutions, while 
promoting the digitalisation of their processes and raising public awareness of the related 
rights. In line with the accession negotiation chapters, it contributes through different 
work areas to several policy fields, such as the free movement of goods, the freedom to 
provide services, consumer protection, market surveillance, intellectual property (IP) 
rights, competition and e-commerce, among others. 
The overall objective is to promote the integration of the Serbian market into the EU 
internal market. The specific objective is to ensure harmonisation with the EU acquis on 
the internal market and to improve the institutional capacity to carry it out (the project fact 
sheet “EU4IM in a nutshell” is attached as Annex II).  
The intervention logic assumes that harmonising national legislation with EU legislation, 
combined with capacity building, outreach on the EU’s work in Serbia and public 
awareness-raising, together with stronger institutional digitalisation, will generate 
verifiable progress in the five chapters and, in the medium term, greater market 
integration. 
The intervention is structured into eight strategic Key Areas of Intervention (KAIs) and 
four cross-cutting components, which are also the outputs of the intervention:

1.2 Context of the evaluation 
Accession process  
Serbia has been a candidate country since 2012 and has been negotiating its accession 
since 2014. It has opened 22 of the 35 chapters of the Stabilisation and Association 
Agreement between Serbia and the EU. Since 2020 the negotiations have been grouped 
into six clusters, and Cluster 2 (Internal Market) covers the intervention areas. Of the five 
chapters covered (1, 3, 7, 8 and 28), only Chapter 7 is open, with an EU common position 
and agreed closing benchmarks. 
When the intervention was designed, the European Commission rated Serbia’s level of 
preparation as moderate in Chapters 1, 3, 8 and 28, and as good in Chapter 7. Since 
then, progress has been limited or non-existent. The Serbia 2025 Report prepared by 
DG ENEST reiterates recommendations from previous years that have not yet been 
implemented, indicating that gaps in alignment and implementation persist. 
The European framework has strengthened the relevance of the intervention. In 
November 2023 the EU adopted the Growth Plan for the Western Balkans and the 
Reform and Growth Facility, endowed with EUR 6 billion for 2024–2027, which makes 
disbursements conditional on the implementation of national reform agendas. Serbia’s 
Reform Agenda, approved in October 2024, includes measures in the intervention areas. 
In April 2025, Serbia also adopted a plan to meet its main accession obligations by the 
end of 2026. 
Political context 
The Government formally maintains accession as a strategic objective. However, anti-
European rhetoric persists in some media and among some public officials, and contacts 
with Russia have intensified. Public trust in the EU is low and knowledge of the internal 
market is limited. This environment may affect the pace of reforms and the ownership of 
their benefits. After two years of numerous citizen protests led by students, elections 
have been called for October 2026. By the time this contract is awarded, a government 
will probably be in the process of being formed; this is a relevant issue for the institutional 
context of the evaluation, with a view to enabling greater participation by the project’s 
partner entities.

3 DURATION, SCOPE AND FRAMEWORK OF THE EVALUATION  
  
Duration: Implementation of the intervention began in November 2023, with an initial 
duration of 36 months. In 2026 a no-cost addendum to the project was processed, 
extending the implementation period until April 2027 and providing for three additional 
months for project closure.   
  
Scope: The evaluation will combine three functions: accountability, learning and capacity 
building of the partner institutions. The performance analysis will be linked to the quality 
of the learning transferred and the autonomy acquired by the counterparts. Therefore, 
results will be assessed not only in terms of the outputs delivered, but also in terms of 
their capacity to generate ownership after the intervention ends.  
  
Criteria and cross-cutting approaches: The six OECD-DAC evaluation criteria, as well as 
EU added value, will be applied to all phases of the intervention, from design and 
implementation through to the monitoring and measurement system.  
  
Methodological approach: Since market integration is a multifactorial, long-term process, 
the evaluation will not measure net effects attributable to the intervention. It will apply 
contribution analysis, testing the intervention logic against contextual dynamics, 
examining the causal assumptions of the results chain and assessing the robustness of 
the evidence available for each link.  
The approach will be predominantly qualitative and, wherever possible, participatory, 
with systematic triangulation between documentary sources, monitoring data and 
information from the stakeholders consulted. The evaluation will encourage the Serbian 
partner institutions to take part in defining the findings and in formulating and prioritising 
the recommendations, in order to strengthen their practical usefulness and ownership.  
  
Evaluation questions: An indicative set of questions to guide the scope of the evaluation 
is attached as Annex I. Based on the desk review, the evaluation team will propose the 
final set in the inception report, together with the judgement criteria, indicators, sources 
and data collection methods. Once the inception report has been approved, the 
questions will be contractually binding.  
  
EU4IM project Activity Information System (SIA): the intervention has an activity 
management tool that organises activities by KAI and component. The tool is the basis 
for the annual narrative reports. The SIA also contains information on institutions and 
participants, as well as on the contribution of the actions to the outputs, with reference 
to indicators and the achievement of targets. The evaluation team will have access to 
the SIA and may use it for its analysis.

ANNEX I Narrative summary of the questions by evaluation criterion 
 
EQ1 — Relevance  
The evaluation should answer the question of the extent to which the design of the 
intervention responded to the needs of the partner institutions on their path towards 
Serbia’s accession to the EU, and whether it remained relevant during implementation. 
To this end, it will first examine whether the eight thematic areas were chosen because 
of their importance for the accession process and the alignment gaps they presented. It 
will also analyse whether the institutions most relevant to advancing the accession 
process were identified, assessing whether the adjustments made were positive, 
appropriate and sufficient.   
The evaluation will examine whether the activities were designed with their effect on the 
progress of negotiations and on institutional preparedness in mind, and whether changes 
to the work plan followed the same logic, responding to changes in context or to unmet 
needs. Finally, it will compare the intervention logic with other models of support for 
integrating domestic markets into the EU market, and will check whether the design 
incorporated the required cross-cutting approaches or, if it did not, whether there were 
requests or resistance from the Serbian counterparts.  
EQ2 — Coherence  
This criterion explores how the intervention fits with other EU and donor support in the 
same areas. It seeks to establish how coordination, synergies and the absence of 
overlaps or duplication with other active projects were ensured, and on what criteria the 
partner institutions distribute their requests among the various projects and donors. In 
addition, it will take a forward-looking view, analysing which results of the intervention 
could be deepened or continued within the funding frameworks planned for the region.  
EQ3 — Effectiveness  
The evaluation should answer the central question of the extent to which the results have 
led to progress in the negotiation chapters and to the necessary changes in the 
performance of the partner institutions, giving its view on the ownership of the Serbian 
counterparts across all the processes of the intervention.   
With regard to the reforms of the legal frameworks supported, it should review whether 
they actually took effect, whether capacities to apply them improved and whether the 
new legal framework is genuinely applicable given the current capacities of the 
institutions supported.   
Another relevant aspect to be analysed will be knowledge transfer: how what was 
learned is reflected in the counterparts’ day-to-day work, and to what extent the 
knowledge became embedded in the institutions rather than merely passed on to a few 
individuals. It will also consider whether the experts selected were the most appropriate 
to make EU knowledge applicable. The actual use of the digital tools delivered will also 
be examined and, finally, which specific advances in the negotiation chapters rest on the 
results of the intervention.

EQ4 — Efficiency  
This criterion assesses whether the management model and the governance structure 
made it possible to contribute to results in an agile, timely and proportionate manner. It 
will analyse whether the division of work among the implementing partners worked, 
whether results were delivered within the planned timeframes and how delays were 
managed, as well as whether the monitoring systems made it possible to measure results 
and guide decisions. The implementation modality will also be compared with others 
used in the country, identifying its advantages and limitations, and the counterparts’ 
views on which modality would be most suitable for future support will be gathered.  
EQ5 — Impact  
This evaluation criterion will seek to determine the level of the intervention’s contribution 
to Serbia’s progress towards integration into the EU internal market. To this end, 
changes in the institutions and in the domestic market that can be linked to the 
intervention will be identified and weighed against other contributing factors, as well as 
against the obstacles that have limited a greater contribution. Attention will be paid to 
unintended effects, both positive and negative, and the evaluation will ask whether the 
intervention has strengthened the entities’ commitment to the accession process and 
whether it has improved public perception of the EU or of the Balkan country’s accession 
process.  
EQ6 — Sustainability  
This criterion asks to what extent the partner institutions have taken ownership of the 
results and how likely they are to be maintained after the intervention ends. It will check 
whether they have made/integrated the necessary regulatory and organisational 
changes, whether they have budget and staff allocated, and whether there are internal 
knowledge-transfer mechanisms to cushion the effect of staff turnover. It will also verify 
whether responsibilities for maintaining and updating the digital tools have been properly 
assigned and embedded, and which non-state actors sustain the demand for the reforms 
supported.

MAIN OBJECTIVES IN EACH KEY AREA OF INTERVENTION (KAI) 
 
KAI 2: MARKET SURVEILLANCE (implementation coordinated by AICS) 
The project addresses some main challenges in the area, such as the alignment of the Strategic and Horizontal 
legal framework with the EU Regulation 2019/1020; the improvement of authorities’ administrative capacity; the 
efficiency in on-line and off-line activities through diagnosis and training; as well as the improvement of the 
performance of laboratory sampling and testing; among others. 
 
KAI 8: QUALITY INFRASTRUCTURE (implementation coordinated by AICS) 
The project supports the preparation of the Agreement on Conformity Assessment and Acceptance of Industrial 
Products (ACAA) between the Republic of Serbia and the EU. It also supports capacity building for relevant 
ministries, institutions and conformity assessment bodies (CABs) to fully implement the Action plans. Some 
provision of software and hardware will be also assessed for this purpose. 
 
KAI 1: E-COMMERCE (implementation coordinated by SAIDC) 
The core achievements will revolve around empowering businesses, startups, and micro-, small and medium-
sized enterprises to seamlessly embrace e-commerce without straining their financial resources. Outcomes 
include e-commerce enablement, implementation of critical EU directives, capacity building for cross-border 
delivery and support to data-driven decision making (statistical methods). 
 
KAI 5: POINT OF SINGLE CONTACT (implementation coordinated by SAIDC) 
Supporting the establishment of Point of Single Contact (PSC) for the services in Serbia, as one of the main 
implications of adoption of the Services Directive, is the aim. It serves as e-government portals designed to 
streamline administrative procedures for service providers, making national information accessible and user-
friendly. 
 
KAI 6: CONSUMER PROTECTION (implementation coordinated by SAIDC) 
The project will support the alignment of consumer protection legislation and policy with the EU acquis; will 
enhance the knowledge and skills of the civil servants, consumer protection organizations, and market regulators 
regarding procedures and obligations. Additionally, it will promote consumer protection awareness and rights 
among the public, as well as support the digitalization of the consumer protection system. 
 
KAI3: INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY RIGHTS (IPR) (implementation coordinated by FIAP) 
By implementing a wide range of activities for IPR enforcers (particularly through the Intellectual Property Office 
– IPO - and the Coordination Body) and IPR holders, the project will support the improvement of relevant 
legislation as well as the administrative and technical institutional capacity. It will also aim at increasing the 
awareness of IPR relevance and enhancing a platform for information sharing on IP enforcement cases. The 
OECD support will provide an assessment of the actual impact of counterfeits and piracy in Serbia, which should 
inform IPO’s structure, mandate and approach. 
 
KAI 4: STATE AID CONTROL (implementation coordinated by FIAP) 
The project aims at harmonizing state rules with the EU acquis, building the capacity of CSAC and raising 
awareness on the importance of state aid control among grantors and beneficiaries. Support to training grantors 
in the e-register platform is also foreseen. 
 
KAI 7: COMPETITION (implementation coordinated by FIAP) 
Built on previous achievements, the project mainly focuses on the improvement of the administrative and technical 
capacity of the CPC and other relevant actors, such as the judiciary dealing with competition cases and the civil 
servants drafting laws and regulations. It will provide training, support to raising awareness initiatives and 
interinstitutional cooperation, as well as enhance forensic investigation technical equipment and software.
